Crew Control

How it works

From scan to action in a few minutes.

Scan the code

A crew member scans a QR code and the check opens in the phone's browser. Nothing to install.

Take the check

Hold still for the camera, fly a short docking mission, answer three questions.

HALO compares and acts

The result is compared with that person's own earlier checks. HALO flags what changed and tells the crew.

Questions

What people ask first.

Is my video recorded?

No. The camera is read on the phone and the video is never saved or sent. Only the resulting numbers are stored, under a random code with no name.

What if the camera cannot get a good reading?

HALO tells you. It coaches you on light and stillness during the scan, and if a signal is still too weak it leaves that reading blank instead of guessing. A fingertip on the camera gives a second way to read the pulse.

How does HALO decide ready, caution or rest?

It combines the camera readings, the reaction tests and your answers into one score, and looks at which readings fall outside their usual range. After a few checks it compares you with your own baseline.

Where does the radiation and space-weather information come from?

From NASA records of the International Space Station and of solar activity, for May 2024. It is shown as historical data and is never mixed with a person's own readings.

Is HALO a medical device?

No. HALO is a prototype. It flags things for a person to look at; it does not diagnose, and its results are not medical advice. The emergency checklists come from published sources and have not been medically reviewed.
